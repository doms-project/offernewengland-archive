Rhode Island specifics

What to know before selling a hoarder house in Rhode Island

Hoarding disorder is a recognized mental-health condition, not a character flaw, and the state treats it that way. The Rhode Island Hoarding Disorder Task Force, convened through the Rhode Island Elder Mental Health and Addiction Coalition, publishes a statewide directory of clean-out services, professional organizers, case managers, and clinical support. If the person who lives in the house is still with you, that directory is a good first call, and selling does not have to be the first step.

When selling is the right step, a few Rhode Island details matter:

Municipal housing codes

Rhode Island cities enforce minimum housing standards locally. A cluttered property can draw a notice for egress, sanitation, or fire-load concerns. An as-is sale to a cash buyer is often the fastest way to resolve an open notice without funding the remediation yourself.

Seller disclosure still applies

Rhode Island requires a written property condition disclosure on most residential sales, even as-is. We do not ask you to hide anything; our offer already assumes the worst, so honest disclosure costs you nothing.

Probate and estate sales

If the house belongs to an estate, the executor or administrator needs authority from the probate court before closing. We can sign an agreement early and close once that authority is in place, which keeps the timeline moving.

Older housing stock

Much of Rhode Island's housing was built before 1978. Long-term clutter in these homes often hides lead paint, knob-and-tube wiring, or moisture damage. We price for it, so you do not have to test for it.

Questions we hear most

Hoarder house FAQ for Rhode Island sellers

Do I have to clean out the house before selling it to you?

No. You can leave everything behind, including furniture, papers, appliances, and personal items you do not want. Take whatever matters to you, and we handle removal after closing. Sellers often ask if they should at least tidy up for the walkthrough; you do not need to. We have seen every level of clutter and it does not change how we treat you.

How do you decide what to offer on a hoarder house?

We look at what the house would be worth after it is cleared and repaired, then subtract the realistic cost of the clean-out, the repairs the clutter is likely hiding, and our holding costs. The number is written down, explained line by line, and there is no obligation to accept it. We would rather lose a deal than surprise you at closing.

Can I sell a hoarder house I inherited if probate is not finished?

Usually, yes. In Rhode Island the executor or administrator needs authority from the probate court to transfer the property, but we can sign a purchase agreement while that is pending and close once the court paperwork is in place. If you are unsure where the estate stands, we can talk through it with you and your attorney at no cost.

What if the city has sent a minimum housing or code violation notice?

Tell us, and send us a copy. An open notice does not stop us from buying. We will factor the required work into our offer and coordinate with the city after closing so the fines stop accruing against you. Many sellers come to us precisely because a notice arrived and they could not afford to comply.

Will you buy a house with pest, mold, odor, or animal damage?

Yes. Rodents, insects, mold, pet damage, and strong odors are common in hoarding situations and are all things we budget for. They affect the offer amount, not whether we make one. You do not need to remediate, test, or disclose anything you are unaware of; you only need to be honest about what you do know.

Can we keep family photos or belongings we have not found yet?

Of course. Before closing, you and your family can take as much time as you need to go through the house. If you find things after closing, call us; we do not clear a property the same day we buy it, and we will make a reasonable effort to set aside personal items like photographs, documents, and heirlooms.

How fast can you close on a hoarder house in Rhode Island?

As fast as 14 days once you accept, because we pay cash and do not need a lender's approval or an appraisal. If you need longer to sort belongings, coordinate with siblings, or wait for probate authority, we set the closing date around you. The timeline is yours, not ours.

Do I still have to fill out Rhode Island's seller disclosure form?

In most residential sales, yes. Rhode Island requires sellers to complete a written property condition disclosure even when the home is sold as-is. It only asks about what you actually know. Because our offer already assumes significant issues, disclosing them does not lower it, and it protects you from claims later.
